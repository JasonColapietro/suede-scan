Reading a readiness report: prioritizing fixes for a Palm Beach Gardens dental or law practice

By Jason Colapietro, founder of Suede AI · November 15, 2026

Picture a family dental office off PGA Boulevard and a two-partner estate planning firm a few blocks away running Suede Audit on the same afternoon. Say the dentist scores 71 and the law firm scores 64: both owners open a report with 28 rows, a dozen of them red. The useful question is not "how do we get to 100" but "which three rows do we fix this week," and the report already holds the answer if you read it in the right order.

Read the points column before the list

Every row in a Suede Audit report carries a severity. A high check is worth five points, a medium check three and a low check one, out of 90 in total, and each failed row names its repair. The rows appear grouped by pillar, Discoverability, Entity evidence and Content clarity, which is a good way to understand a site and a poor way to schedule work. Sort the failures by points instead. One failed high check costs about 5.6 points of the score, so a single blocked crawler outweighs a missing favicon, a missing twitter:card and an image without alt text put together.

Jason Colapietro, who founded Suede AI in West Palm Beach in 2024 and wrote the scoring rules, gives professional practices one more filter: fix anything that stops a crawler from reaching or indexing the page before anything that changes how the page reads. A beautiful service page that answers noindex to Googlebot is invisible, and no amount of copywriting changes that.

Where Palm Beach Gardens practice sites usually fail

Dental and law sites in north Palm Beach County tend to fail in the same few places, and the reasons are specific to how those sites get built.

A blocked or broken robots.txt. Practice sites often sit behind a security plugin or a CDN bot filter added after a spam scare. If /robots.txt answers with a 403 or times out, all four scored crawler checks fail at once, which is 20 points. Suede Signal's guide to which AI crawlers to allow walks through the search crawlers versus the training crawlers, and the audit's crawler access reference shows exactly how each rule is read.

Schema that names the specialty but not the entity. Many dental themes emit "@type": "Dentist", and many legal themes emit LegalService or the older Attorney type, which schema.org now flags as superseded by LegalService. Those are real schema.org types, but the audit's entity check looks for Organization, Corporation, LocalBusiness, OnlineBusiness or Person by name. The fix keeps the specialty and adds the parent in a type array, such as "@type": ["Dentist", "LocalBusiness"], which the scanner reads correctly and which stays accurate to schema.org's Dentist definition. Then add sameAs links to the Google Business Profile and the practice's LinkedIn page. Use areaServed for the towns patients actually drive in from, such as Palm Beach Gardens, Jupiter, Juno Beach and North Palm Beach, and leave the street address in your Business Profile, where it can be verified.

Booking and intake widgets that replace copy. A new-patient form or a consultation scheduler often loads in an iframe or through JavaScript, and on some homepages it is most of the page. The content checks want 300 words of server-rendered text and two descriptive H2 sections, and a widget contributes none of it. Keep the widget, but put real paragraphs above it: what you treat or which matters you handle, who you serve, and how a first visit or consultation works.

A triage order for the first two weeks

Here is the order Jason Colapietro recommends for a Palm Beach Gardens practice reading its first report, and the order the Suede team follows when it does readiness work.

  1. Access, day one. Clear any failure on HTTP status, HTTPS, noindex, the mobile viewport or the four search crawlers. Each is a high check, and most are a settings change rather than a rebuild.
  2. Identity, day two. Make sure one JSON-LD block parses, declares the practice with a type the audit recognizes, and carries sameAs. That is 13 points in three rows, and the structured data reference lists every pass rule.
  3. Readable copy, week one. Fix the single H1, add H2 sections for services or practice areas, reach 300 words, and link at least three internal pages, such as the team page, a services page and the contact page.
  4. Chunking, week two. Break any section over 375 words. A law firm's "About our firm" block that runs 600 words under one heading is the most common offender, and a split also helps patients and clients skim.
  5. Low checks last. Favicon, Open Graph image, twitter:card, html lang and image alt text are one point each. Batch them into a single sitting.

Re-run the audit after each step, the way Suede does on client work. The report caches per browser, so the cleanest check is the before-and-after of the same rows rather than the headline number alone.

What the report leaves to you

A readiness report reads one homepage and three root files. It does not read your implant page, your probate page or your reviews, and it cannot tell you whether ChatGPT recommends you for "estate planning attorney in Palm Beach Gardens." Those are the next layers. Law firms should also run any new page copy past The Florida Bar's lawyer advertising rules before publishing, and dental offices should keep treatment claims as plain as their consent forms. Once the site is readable, some practices add an after-hours intake assistant; Agentix's note on where AI agents help and fail for West Palm Beach and Jupiter service businesses is a sober place to start that conversation.
