Notes from Suede AI

The five fixes we make first on West Palm Beach and Jupiter business websites

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO of Suede AI · November 14, 2026

Most business websites between Boca Raton and Tequesta were built to look good on a phone and win a spot in the map pack. Answer engines read them differently, and the repairs that change how ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI features handle a local site are usually small, cheap and invisible to the owner. These are the five fixes Suede AI makes first when we take on a Palm Beach County site, in the order we make them.

1. Open robots.txt to the search crawlers

Nothing else matters if the crawler is turned away at the door. Suede Audit scores four search-discovery crawlers: OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and Googlebot. A missing robots.txt that returns 404 blocks nothing. A security plugin or a CDN bot rule that answers /robots.txt with a 403, a 5xx or a timeout is worse than no file at all, because the audit cannot confirm access and all four crawler checks fail.

The fix takes ten minutes. Serve a plain-text file with a 200 status, allow the search crawlers you want answering questions about you, add a Sitemap: line, and decide separately whether training crawlers such as GPTBot and Google-Extended should read the site. The crawler access reference shows how each rule is read.

2. Say who you are in LocalBusiness schema

A homepage that says "Welcome!" in a hero banner tells an engine nothing about which business it is. JSON-LD fixes that. Mark up the homepage with the most specific type that fits, such as LocalBusiness, ProfessionalService or Dentist, and include your name, URL, logo and an areaServed list of the towns you actually serve: West Palm Beach, Jupiter, Palm Beach Gardens, Juno Beach, wherever you work.

Then add sameAs links to your Google Business Profile, LinkedIn company page and the directories where you are listed. Keep the business name and city spelled the same in every one of them. Engines corroborate a business across independent sources, and a mismatch between "Smith Roofing LLC" here and "Smith Roofing & Solar" there is a reason to hesitate. The structured data reference lists what the audit requires, and Suede AI publishes the same kind of Organization schema, with sameAs links, on every page of this site.

3. Write titles and descriptions a buyer would recognize

The audit passes a title between 15 and 60 characters and a description between 50 and 160. Inside those limits, lead with the service and the place, the way a buyer phrases a question: "Roof Repair in Jupiter, FL" beats "Home | Excellence in Every Shingle." Add Open Graph tags and a twitter:card, because a link pasted into a group text or a neighborhood forum renders from those tags. Details are on the metadata reference; every Suede page, this one included, keeps its title inside those limits.

4. Put real words in the HTML, under real headings

Some page builders send an almost empty HTML shell and paint the copy in with JavaScript. A person sees a full page. A retrieval crawler that does not run scripts sees nothing to quote. The content checks want one H1, at least two descriptive H2 sections, 300 or more words of server-rendered text, three internal links and an alt attribute on every image. Jason Colapietro's rule of thumb for a Palm Beach County contractor: 300 words is one honest paragraph each on what you do, where you do it, and how a job works. The fix is to server-render the copy, the first change Suede AI makes when it rebuilds a page-builder site. See the content structure reference.

5. Keep every section short enough to quote

Retrieval systems cut pages into chunks of roughly 500 tokens, about 375 words. When a section runs longer, a claim near its end can be lifted without the heading that gives it meaning. Give each service, each town and each common question its own heading, and keep the answer under that heading tight. An FAQ that answers "Do you serve Tequesta?" in two sentences is more useful to an answer engine than a 900-word About page. The retrieval chunk reference explains the measurement, and it is built the same way Suede structures every reference page on this site.
