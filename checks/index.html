Check reference

Every check in the audit, and the rule behind it.

A Suede Audit report runs 28 weighted checks against one public homepage and three root discovery files. This reference documents each one: the signal it reads, the exact rule the scanner applies, why search engines and AI crawlers care, and how to clear a failure. Every rule is read from the open scoring code.

How the weights add up

Each check carries a severity. High counts 5 points, medium 3 and low 1. The 28 checks add up to 90 points: 10 high checks worth 50, 11 medium checks worth 33, and 7 low checks worth 7. The readiness score is the share of those points a page earns, rounded to a whole number. One failed high check costs about 5.6 points of the score, a medium check about 3.3, and a low check about 1.1.

Checks roll up into three pillars, scored the same way: Discoverability (13 checks, 49 points), Entity evidence (3 checks, 13 points) and Content clarity (12 checks, 28 points). Grades use the same bands everywhere: A from 90, B from 80, C from 70, D from 55, and F below 55. The method page covers what the audit fetches and how results are cached.

Reported beside the score

Three crawler tokens that control model training or AI use, GPTBot, ClaudeBot and Google-Extended, are read from the same robots.txt with the same rule and shown in their own panel. They never change the score; the crawler access page explains why. The evidence summary, which lists the title, description, language, canonical, schema types, image alt counts and sitemap entries the audit found, is reported without scoring too.
